	Pros: 
		Activision-Blizzard offers a few perks to employees, such as free games that they publish, performance-based bonuses, and the occasional "care package" or invitation to company-sponsored holiday events.


	Cons: 
		The company leaves some of its departments understaffed, resulting in more work to be done than can possibly be handled and sets performance expectations at unreasonable levels. For example, a team might have a task that can sometimes be completed in 5 minutes, but other times might require 20 to finish, but team members will be expected to meet a quota as if the task only takes 7 minutes to complete on average, which is only possible to achieve when the vast majority of the tasks that come in are of the 5 minute variety, but that is rarely the case.
		
		These practices lead to sloppy work, and some employees spend more time finding ways to exploit the system to make their numbers look good without actually doing any of the work.
		
		Couple that with the annual layoffs that often see the best employees being shown the door while the worst are kept on because their numbers look good, and the perennial bouts of corporate restructuring that eliminate jobs and drop the workloads of those positions onto others, and you are left with a high-stress job and no sense of security.


	Summary: 
		Layoffs Abound

	Pros: 
		Everyone I work with, directly and indirectly, has proven to be friendly, talented, and wonderfully experienced at what they do. The company is great at providing technical resources to support work—laptops, phones, software, etc—and it never feels like reasonable asks are rejected. Compensation and benefits are strong. Conduct expectations are high. Strategic goals are clear. And whether they're gamers or not, everyone is excited about the work they do and who they're doing it for—you simply can't replicate that.


	Cons: 
		The decentralized structure of the company fosters power struggles, crossed incentives, and conflicting efforts to pursue common goals. Work flexibility is entirely dependent on your specific team. The pathways to resources, technical or otherwise, are overly complex and difficult to learn for new employees. The negative moments in the company's recent history seem to weigh on everything and everyone, creating a tremendously risk-averse environment that prioritizes perceived liability over customer satisfaction. And the pending merger with Microsoft has encouraged short-term tactics at the risk of confident, long-term strategy.


	Summary: 
		Tremendous talent, chaotic org

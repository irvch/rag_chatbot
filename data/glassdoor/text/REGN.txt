	Pros: 
		- There are two kinds of employees in manufacturing - ones that drink the koolaid and ones that don’t. If you find yourself on a team with your kind of people, then that will increase your likelihood of not having a crappy day at work because you’ll be spending at least 40 hours a week with those people.
		
		- As someone who really benefits from weekly therapy sessions, I’ve really valued the health benefits thus far as my insurance leaves me with no copay, making every session completely free to me.
		
		- The commute from where I live (in downtown Albany) is about 5 minutes, and about 15 minutes when I lived in downtown Troy.


	Cons: 
		- The promise of ease of movement within the company is a blatant lie. Especially if you start in manufacturing, it is notoriously difficult to move to another department to further your professional development - either you’re doing too poor of a job that no one else will want you, or you’re doing too good of a job that they don’t want to have to replace you. 
		
		- Unsurprisingly, once you start to struggle with maintaining a healthy work/life balance, management and HR will pounce at any sign of weakness and use it as an excuse to withhold promotions. They’ll blow smoke up your butt and commend you for going “above and beyond” and being a “go-to person” for any extra work they need doing, but will feign concern and empathy when you need support and accommodations. 
		
		- Little is ever done to alleviate concrete issues between two employees working on the same shift, and the overwhelming mindset at Regeneron is that everyone should be able to work with everyone or you’re deemed a “problem employee”. I’ve had to endure over a year of gaslighting and antagonistic behavior from a male coworker who’s work performance has proven to be regularly subpar (while mine is constantly commending for being high), and when this quickly began to take a toll on my mental health, I was invalidated time and time again. My honesty was used against me, I was told I was making excuses (which made me afraid to even be forthright with the issues that inevitably continued), and my (25 year old) HR BP went so far as to bring me into a meeting to tell me that “it seems like [I’m] on the verge of a mental health crisis” to which her solution was an e-mail to me with copy and pasted “resources” (most of which were so obviously not applicable to me at all) so that she could check a box off and convince herself that she had done her job well. 
		
		-If someone, especially someone in your management chain, doesn’t like you - RUN. Move to a difference shift, a different department, or a different company altogether because a target has been painted on your back and nothing good will come from you staying where you are. It really is about who you know at Regeneron, and even more so who you’re friends with. Word travels fast, including personal and biased accounts of you through the lens of whomever has a problem with you.
		
		- As soon as you make it known that you aren’t completely and 100% satisfied with everything, you fall into the category of the “red, frowny face people” (current employees should get this). Once that happens, the powers that be could not care less what you have to say, and are basically waiting for you to leave. If you leave Regeneron for any reason, good or bad, you are dead to them.

		The Rose Colored Glasses Came Off Years Ago…
